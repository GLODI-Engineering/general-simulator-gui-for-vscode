## 2026-11-20 10:13 — the previous app leaves the repository

The project is three things — this repository draws and sends, `gsim-core` compiles for a
target, `general-simulator` solves — and most of what was in `docs/` described neither the first
of those nor anything that still exists. 65 files and 2.6 MB went, archived outside the
repository first at `omni-schematics-legacy-2026-11-20/` with a note explaining every group.

**Orphaned media and dead code.** `docs/screenshots/` (two PNGs of the standalone app, one
showing a block labelled "Generic Switch (Xyce)", referenced by nothing); `docs/circuit_test/`
(1.7 MB: two different old toolbars, two unreferenced `.webm` recordings, ngspice-against-Xyce
matplotlib plots, and four `.npy` binaries committed as build artifacts of a removed backend);
`simulator-compat.yaml` and `scripts/check-simulator-compat.mjs`, which #63 recorded as
unreadable-by-anything and un-wired to CI. The YAML had to go in the same commit as the four
documents instructing contributors to edit it — an earlier attempt was backed out for exactly
that reason, and this time `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s line went with it.

**Documentation of a product that no longer exists.** `docs/user-guide.md`, whose shortcut table
is entirely VS Code's own keys (#73); `frontend-spec.md` (1,331 lines) and `backend-spec.md`
(910) specifying the standalone app and the pre-engine sidecar; `simulator-architecture-spec.md`;
`docs/gsim-format.md` (the v1 `.gsim`); the two simulator-component documents; and
`docs/spice-simulator-analsys/` — seven files of ngspice 46 and Xyce 7.10 catalogues.

**Research and concept notes**, on the owner's decision: that reasoning now belongs to
`general-simulator`, which is mature and heavily tested. `dae_ode_coupling_hybrid_simulation.md`,
`pipeline_IR_design.md`, `simulator_concept.md`, `visualization_specification.md`,
`block-diagram-gui-analysis.md`.

**`docs/agent-rules/orientation.md` went too**, which was not in the survey. It is a navigation
guide *to* the deleted specs — 45 references to them, a reading order through frontend-spec and
backend-spec, and a 30-row "source of truth" table pointing at their sections. An index cannot
outlive what it indexes. `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`'s reading order now points at the invariants, the
LLC article, and `gsim-core` for anything about what a schematic is.

**Two files the survey got wrong, and they stayed.** `docs/specs/keyboard-map.md` reads like a
spec for the old app and is live infrastructure: the `keymap-drift` CI job parses its chord

**Two schematics were rescued rather than deleted.** `buck_converter.gsim_sch` and

uses — `render-schematic.mjs` documents one as its example and `test_schema_sync.py` round-trips

move ("Buck converter — direct ngspice netlist" → "Buck converter").

**Where a deleted spec was load-bearing, the substance moved into the code** rather than leaving
a pointer to nothing: the solver path-discovery chain is now documented in `path_discovery.py`
itself, and the HDF5 LOD table says it is the only record of itself. Each architecture invariant
already stated its own reasoning in full, so the `Source:` lines dropped cleanly.

Every drift gate still passes — keymap, keybinding, schema codegen, validators, invariants,
engine catalogue — and so do 1,470 TypeScript tests and 320 Python tests. The one gate that
caught a real mistake was `gen-validators:check`: editing a schema description left the compiled
validator stale.

Not done here, and the reason this is only half the job: the view repository still emits
netlists, in `apps/frontend/src/netlist/` (TypeScript) and
`packages/simulator/src/simulator/compiler/` (Python). That is code, not prose, and it gets its

## 2026-11-12 (late night) — terminals in thin air, and an ideal transformer at last

**Repos:** `gsim-core` `177f91e` `0d628af` · `general-mna` `8e21842` ·
`general-simulator-gui-for-vscode` `8895c18` `9ca2754`

All of this started from two phone photographs: port markers sitting beside a
component with nothing joining them, and a rectifier drawn as four diodes in a
column with wires looping around it.

### Terminals that did not touch their symbol

Four components declared ports no artwork could reach. A symbol is drawn inside
its bbox, so a port on a face *corner* can only ever draw detached.

- `source.dependent.cccs` / `.ccvs` — **both** terminals on the right face at
  offsets 1 and 59 of 60, while the diamond's leads leave left and right at
  mid-height. Now left 30 / right 30.
- `physical_electrical.opamp.comparator` — `vn` at offset 40 of a 40-tall face.
  Those are `opamp.ideal`'s offsets, whose box is 60 tall; in a 40-tall box the
  lower input lands outside a triangle that ends at y=38. Now 14 and 26.
- `semi.mesfet.n` — gate, drain and source all at the far corner of their faces.

Plus two artwork faults in the same diamonds: centred at `cy = 40` in a 60-tall
box, and a `currentSense` arrow drawn from y=66 to y=80 — outside the box,
colliding with the value label, and implying a wired connection a
current-controlled source does not have (it names its controlling element in
`control`).

**The gate:** `gen-engine-catalog.mjs` now rejects any port within two units of
a face corner. Four violations before, zero after.

### "It was impossible to draw a line and touch a line"

Correct, and worth recording precisely: **wire tapping exists on `main` and is
not in `v0.2.0`**, which is what is installed. `wire-tap.ts` landed 2026-11-09.
The feature was reported as missing because the released build does not have it.

And it would not have worked anyway: the editor splices a `routing.junction`
block in when a branch lands on a conductor, and **the compile registry had no
entry for that type**, so every tapped schematic failed on Run with
`unknown-type`. Now registered as structural — no card emitted, and the net
walk merges the conductors meeting at its `node` port. Verified on the LLC:
four conductors joined through a junction give the same node and the same
48.547 V as the port-to-port version.

### The ideal transformer, and the solver bug under it

An ideal transformer is now a reusable `.SUBCKT` — a VCVS, a 0 V sense source
and a CCCS — placed as **one block** with `p1 p2 s1 s2`.

It could not have been, until today. An `F`/`H` card names the voltage source
whose branch current controls it, and that name is scoped to the body it is
written in. `general-mna`'s flattening prefixed every element name with the
instance path and left the reference alone, so `F1 p1 p2 VS 0.25` expanded
still pointing at `VS` while the source became `X1.VS`:
`unknown controlling branch 'VS'`. `raw_params[0]` is the controlling element
for exactly those two device letters, and is now prefixed with them.

Checked standalone (100 V, 4:1, 10 Ω → 25.0000 V, 0.625 A, 62.5 W both sides)
and then inside the LLC, where it reproduces the inline version to the
millivolt.

### The schematic, redrawn

The rectifier is two legs. Each return has its **own** ground symbol — with one
shared symbol every return is a separate wire to the same point and the router
draws them as a ribbon of parallel lines across the sheet, which is most of
what made the old figure unreadable. And the bleeders stay: I expected an ideal
diode's 1e-9 S off-state to define the floating secondary and tested it —
without them the output is 43.4 V with 25 V ripple instead of 48.5 V with 0.12 V.

### Verification

`gsim-core` 194 lib tests, `general-mna` 43 (two new), frontend 1279, Python
296 with the same two pre-existing failures. biome, typecheck, ruff, mypy and
check-invariants clean. The engine was rebuilt from source at 0.2.0 and
reproduces the committed catalogue byte for byte before the port fixes were
applied — which is what made it safe to touch the component library at all.

### Still open

`auto_arrange` is unusable for a figure (this layout is hand-placed), and
declared parameter defaults are still rejected as missing by the compiler.

## 2026-11-12 (night) — the LLC article, and what writing it found

**Branch:** `chore/general-simulator-only` — commit `7ca659f`
**Article:** `docs/articles/simulating-an-llc-converter.md`

A newcomer-level walk-through: configure the extension, derive the LLC gain
law, build the converter, simulate, and compare against theory. Written as a
test of the tool as much as of the writing.

### It works, and the agreement is the right shape

| $f_n$ | FHA | simulated | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.80 | 53.61 V | 54.56 V | +1.77% |
| 0.95 | 49.70 V | 49.67 V | **−0.06%** |
| 1.00 | 48.80 V | 48.55 V | −0.52% |
| 1.30 | 45.31 V | 43.36 V | −4.30% |

Smallest at resonance, growing in both directions — the textbook limitation of
first-harmonic approximation, reproduced by a solver that knows nothing about
FHA. Agreement *everywhere* would have been the suspicious result.

Also verified: ZVS from the sign of the tank current at turn-on (−1.178 A);
magnetising-current peak 1.387 A against 1.347 A analytical; the ideal
transformer macro (VCVS + 0 V sense + CCCS) exact on voltage, current and
power before being used; and `kind=measure` against Python post-processing
agreeing to nine significant figures on the same solve.

### Five defects, all in the article rather than hidden

1. **`auto_arrange` is not presentable** for circuits with heavy ground
   fan-out — long crossing wires, scattered parts. The figure is hand-placed.
2. **A dependent source's value label collides with its own symbol** (the CCCS
   draws "250 m" over the diamond).
3. **Declared parameter defaults are rejected as missing.** The catalogue
   gives every parameter a default; the compiler still demands each one
   (`missing-param: block 'S1' is missing required parameter 'g_breakdown'`).
   The editor fills them at insert time, so this bites generated files only —
   but a default that cannot be relied on is a sharp edge.
4. **No ideal-transformer primitive.** Four blocks and a sign convention for
   something every isolated topology needs.
5. **A floating rectifier secondary needs bleeders**, and fails as a singular
   instant rather than a clear error.

### Loose end closed

`generalSimulator.ngspicePath` and `.xycePath` removed from the extension's
contributed settings. Declared but unread since those solvers went; a tutorial
naming them would send a newcomer after something that does nothing.

## 2026-11-12 (end of day, later) — one domain vocabulary, and ten blocks that had the wrong one

**Branch:** `chore/general-simulator-only` — commit `e6b1a0d`

The two engine-side gaps reported in the previous entry turned out to be one
front-end bug and one contract defect, both fixable here.

### The bug

`inferDomain` existed twice — in `library/insert.ts` and in
`inspector/ComponentParamsPanel.tsx` — and the copies had drifted. Both
guessed a placed block's domain from its first port, then from
`behavior.domain`. Neither read `metadata.domain`, the field the engine
declares and the schema requires.

Measured against the engine's 239 components, the guess disagreed with the
declaration for **ten**:

| component | declared | guessed |
|---|---|---|
| `converter.sig2phys` | mixed | physical-electrical |
| `converter.phys2sig` | mixed | signal |
| `physical_translational.sensor.{force,position,velocity}` | physical-translational | physical-signal |
| `physical_electrical.semiconductor.photodiode` | physical-electrical | physical-signal |
| `hierarchy.subsystem.{enabled,triggered}` | mixed | signal |
| `routing.bus_{assignment,creator}` | signal | signal-bus |

Every one straddles two domains — precisely when a first-port heuristic must
fail, because no single port speaks for the block. That is why the block
declares a domain of its own. One `blockDomainOf` now, reading the
declaration; the port fallback survives only for a component that declares
none, and `behavior.domain` is not consulted at all.

This was never going to fail a test: domain drives wire colour, port symbols
and the causal/acausal split, so a wrong one draws a sheet that misstates the
connection rather than breaking.

### The contract defect

Three vocabularies for one concept:

- a block's `domain` had no enum of its own — it `$ref`s `PortKindDomain`
- `PortKindDomain` accepted `physical-electrical` *and* `electrical`,
  `physical-thermal` *and* `thermal`, plus `mechanical` and `event`
- `behavior.domain` is a third enum entirely

`mechanical` is the one that cannot be translated: it collapses
`physical-rotational` and `physical-translational`, and every bridge resolved
it to translational, so the 13 rotational components lost their domain
wherever that path ran. The short spellings are now illegal — nothing used
them, and all 239 components still satisfy the tightened schema. `v2Domain`
and the identity port pass it fed are gone.

`behavior.domain` keeps its short vocabulary because the engine emits it, but
nothing in the front end reads it for a domain now. **Unifying it is a
gsim-core change and is still open.**

### Verification

13 new tests, including one that walks the whole engine catalogue and asserts
`blockDomainOf` agrees with every declaration. Frontend 1279 passed;
typecheck, biome, check-invariants clean.

Side effect worth knowing: a public repo gets unlimited standard-runner CI
minutes.

### The three gaps, closed

All three were the same shape — the contract was honoured by convention and
never checked, so where the two halves disagreed, nothing said so.

**1. The engine's submission is now validated.** `gen-engine-catalog.mjs`
checks every component against `block-metadata.schema.json` before writing.
The drift check asks *did the answer change*; this asks *is the answer legal*.
All 239 pass today. Proved it bites by pointing `GSIM_PYTHON` at a fake engine
submitting a component with no label and an illegal paradigm — exit 1, naming
the component and the field.

**2. An alias no longer means two different things.** Three parts of the app
disagreed about whether an alias counts:

| | resolves aliases? |
|---|---|
| `engine-types.ts` (`supportsBlock`) | yes |
| `catalog-core.ts` (the metadata the canvas draws from) | **no** |
| gsim-core's netlist emitter | **no** |

So an aliased document was reported compilable, drew as an unnamed box, and
failed at compile with `unknown-type` naming a spelling the user never typed.
`canonicalizeBlockTypes` now rewrites every block — at every depth, subsystem
bodies included — to the canonical name inside `deserialize`, the single door
every document enters through. The catalogue resolves aliases too, in a second
pass that cannot shadow a real component's name.

**3. The front end is now tested against a catalogue gsim-core did not write.**
`foreign-catalog.test.ts` invents components, proves they satisfy the contract
by validating them against the schema, then asserts the loader reads them
faithfully. Before this, every catalogue test ran on the real engine output,
which proves the app works with one supplier and cannot prove anything more.

Both engine-side, reported not fixed:

- `behavior.domain` is an enum of `signal/electrical/thermal/mechanical/event/mixed`

## 2026-11-12 (later still) — general-simulator is the only backend

**Branch:** `chore/general-simulator-only` — commit `b8cb880`
**Recovery tag:** `archive/python-compiler`

### The decision

The backend is general-simulator. The front end draws the schematic and owns
it; the engine submits the catalogue of what it has; gsim-core compiles the
document to a netlist; general-simulator solves it; the app displays the
result. That is the whole contract, and everything serving a different
backend is outside it.

### Why so much Python was here

An earlier design had this repository *be* the simulator. Every component
carried an `equations.py`; those lowered through ir1 → ir2 into either a
SciPy state-space problem or a SPICE netlist for ngspice or Xyce. That
design needed a second block taxonomy maintained by hand beside the
engine's, which is where the 26 + 11 disagreements measured in the previous
entry came from. The split was in the wrong place: a component's behaviour
belongs to the engine that solves it, not to the app that draws it.

442 tracked `.py` → 128. What is left is not a simulator:

| | |
|---|---|
| `apps/kernel-sidecar` (46) | the runtime bridge. A webview cannot spawn a process or call a Rust library, so the extension spawns `python -m kernel_sidecar`, which speaks the IPC protocol, calls `gsim_py` to compile, runs the binary and streams waveform chunks back. |
| `packages/simulator` (37) | load/validate a `.gsim_sch`, flatten it, resolve goto/from, compile via `gsim_py_netlist`, run the binary, parse its CSV, write results. Plumbing, not solving. |
| `packages/gsim-model` (19) | the `.gsim_sym` component format and schematic layout geometry. |

It is Python rather than TypeScript because `gsim_py` is a Python binding to
the Rust core. A Node binding, or a JSON CLI on gsim-core, would let the
sidecar be Node and the Python disappear entirely.

### Removed

`packages/blocks` (265 `metadata.yaml` + 234 equation modules),
`packages/engine-xyce`, the ir1/ir2/SciPy/SPICE pipeline and every `*_lower`
pass, `dim_propagate`, `dae_system`, `mna/`, `statespace/`, `behavioral/`,
`symbolic/`, `equation_templates/`, `selection/`, the ngspice/Xyce/SciPy
solvers, and the raw-`netlist` + `solver=` request paths whose only purpose
was reaching them. `simulate.py`'s four-way dispatch is one path.

598 files, −36,345 lines.

### Two things the cut got wrong at first, both caught by tests

- `goto_from_resolve` was deleted with the compiler. It is schematic
  routing, not compilation — `gsim_model.connectivity` needs it. Restored.
- `gsim_model.catalog` read port geometry from `packages/blocks`. Rewritten
  to read `gsim_py.catalog()`, whose entries carry `symbol.bbox` and
  `ports.{inputs,outputs}[].{id,side,offset}` in the same shape. A
  `blocks_root` override stays for an *authored* component directory, which
  no engine knows about yet.

### Verification

Python 296 passed / 2 failed — the same two that failed before this branch
(`test_smoke_e2e`, `test_xfail_circuit`), and **no xfail left**: 73 of them
were "not yet implemented" markers on modules now simply gone. Frontend
1271 passed. ruff, ruff format, mypy, biome, check-invariants clean.
`uv sync --all-packages` resolves after dropping `blocks` from the
`simulator` and `synthesis` dependency lists.

Note: `uv sync` uninstalls `gsim_py` and `gsim_tools`, which are
hand-installed rather than locked. Pre-existing, not caused here, but it
bites after any sync — reinstall both before running anything.

### Still legacy, not touched here

`apps/frontend/src/inspector/device-models.ts` is ~1,700 lines of ngspice
and Xyce device-model tables. Inert — `allowedModelLevels` returns `[]` for
general-simulator — but it is the same kind of second description, and the
simulator picker still offers backends that no longer exist.

## 2026-11-12 (later) — the old catalogue is not dead, and the numbers say how far it has drifted

**Branch:** `chore/remove-the-dead-catalog` — commit `d7a9403`

### What was asked

Remove the old catalogue. It is superseded by `engine-catalog.json` and is
exactly the kind of leftover that makes a later session pick the wrong
source of truth — the same failure mode as the two GUI repos.

### What was found, and the correction that matters

It is not dead. The front end no longer reads it, which is what made it
look dead, but `packages/blocks/**/metadata.yaml` is still a **runtime**
input to the Python compiler in two places:

- `compiler/dim_propagate.py:95` builds `blocks_root / path_part /
  "metadata.yaml"` and reads `port_sigs` off disk to seed signal-dimension
  propagation. For any block, not only the ones with `equations.py`.
- `equation_templates/loader.py` reads `behavior.equation_template` for the
  91 directories that do have an `equations.py`.

Deleting the 174 "catalogue-only" directories took the Python suite from
**3 failed / 709 passed to 256 failed / 448 passed**. The deletion was
reverted.

Method note, because it caused the wrong call: the grep that "proved" only
tests read `metadata.yaml` was piped through `head -25` and returned
exactly 25 lines. `dim_propagate.py` was the 26th. A truncated search was
read as a complete one. Searches that decide whether something can be
deleted get counted, not skimmed.

### The drift, measured

Comparing `gsim_py.catalog()` against the 265 YAML files, every YAML block
exists in the engine (0 missing) — but the two disagree:

- **26 blocks** have a different `behavior.equation_template`
- **11 blocks** have different `port_sigs`
- 67 YAML carry `port_sigs`; the engine carries 56

So the GUI and the Python compiler are today describing 26 blocks
differently. That is the drift the one-catalogue decision exists to end,
and it is already real rather than hypothetical.

### What was removed

Only what is unambiguously dead, each verified in isolation against the
baseline:

- `simulator/catalogue/` — scans `*_ideal.py` / `*_behavioral.py` /
  `*_spice.py` and builds a registry no production code imports. Its only
  consumer was `test_catalogue_v2_coverage.py`, a test written for it.
- The first loop in `equation_templates/loader.py` — walked 174 YAML files
  per load to reach a `logger.debug` call and nothing else.

After: 3 failed / 701 passed, the same three pre-existing failures.

### What it will take to finish

The engine already carries all three fields the Python side needs
(`equation_template` and `state_count` for all 239, `port_sigs` for 56).
So the end state is reachable: point `dim_propagate` and the template
loader at `engine-catalog.json`, then delete the YAML. The blocker is not
plumbing, it is the 26 + 11 disagreements — each needs a decision about
which description is correct, and that is engine semantics, not a refactor.

### Also still open

- `simulator-compat.ts` hand-lists 59 types inline while
  `engine-capabilities.json` carries 61 generated from the engine.
- `gen-engine-catalog.mjs` does not validate the engine's submission
  against the block-metadata schema, so the contract is unenforced.
- Nothing tests the GUI against a catalogue that did not come from
  gsim-core, so "displays faithfully whatever conforms" is unproven.

## 2026-11-12 — a subsystem's ports follow its body, and the render gate was not a gate

**Branch:** `feat/subsystem-ports-follow-the-body` — commits `7448510`, `21416e3`
**Issue:** #44

### What was asked

Make the parent-side subsystem block reconstruct its input and output ports
automatically when the body changes, and make boundary ports easier to see:
they were small and thin next to real components and hard to pick out.

### What was found

Two bugs, the second hiding the first.

`syncSubsystemPorts` was only ever called at `Ctrl+G`. After the group was
formed nothing re-derived the ports, so a boundary port added, renamed or
deleted inside the body left the parent block unchanged — the sheet looks
right and no longer connects.

Underneath it, `setParam` resolved block ids against `document.model` only.
Editing a port's `id` or `kind` from inside a body therefore did nothing at
all, so there was no rename for the sync to propagate even if it had run.
Fixing only the first bug would have shipped a sync with nothing to sync,
and it would have looked correct in any test that built the document by
hand instead of going through the store.

### What was done

`setParam` is hierarchy-aware, with a root fallback so a root block stays
editable by id while the user is inside a body. `mutateModelAtPath` calls
`syncAncestorSubsystemPorts`, which walks the path deepest-first so a
two-level edit settles in one pass. Parent wires follow a renamed port by
its `boundary_block` identity and are dropped when the port goes. 11 tests
in `subsystem-ports-follow-body.test.ts` pin the wiring rather than the
derivation, including the store paths that were the actual break.

Artwork: marks take a 1.6x stroke (`MARK_SW` ≈ 3.07 against `SW` = 1.92),
leads stay at wire weight, and the causal flag is filled in its domain
colour. Height could not grow — the 200x40 bbox and the port offset come
from `engine-catalog.json`, which is generated from `gsim_py.catalog()`
behind a CI drift gate, so a taller boundary port is a gsim-core change
first. Width and weight were the free axes.

### The gate that could not fail

`scripts/render-schematic.mjs` is the tool invariant 6 names, and it never
built anything — `vite preview` serves `apps/frontend/dist` off disk. The
bundle on disk was from 2026-11-09. Three renders of a changed symbol came
back byte-identical, and the PNG was read closely and judged ("the lead is
too heavy, a lead is just a wire") — a judgement about code that was not in
the picture. The conclusion happened to be right, which is worse: it means
the procedure can be followed exactly and still certify nothing.

The same run exposed a second bug. The script hung after writing its PNG,
for twenty-five minutes, with no output. `pnpm` is not the server: it spawns
`sh -c vite preview`, which spawns the node process that binds the port.
Killing the pnpm pid orphaned that grandchild, which held the port and the
script's stdio pipes — so the process never exited, and the next render
silently reused the stale server it had left behind. That is why the hashes
kept matching.

Both fixed in `21416e3`: unconditional build before serving (~4s warm,
`--no-build` to opt out), preview spawned detached and the whole process

## 2026-11-05 (later) — rendering made grid-exact, a .vsix that installs, and a Python front end

**Branch:** `main` — commits `44f75d3`, `b689edf`, `120b291`

### Rendering

Every fix in `44f75d3` was found by rendering the buck converter and looking
at it, not by a failing test. The root cause of most of the geometry defects
was one thing: `SYMBOL_SCALE` 1.6 and `STUB_LEN` 16 against a `GRID` of 20,
so a port centre landed at 32 and a two-terminal part spanned 128 — nothing
on grid, ever. At scale 2 with a one-grid lead the arithmetic closes and
every terminal sits on an intersection. `wire-path.ts`'s six-pixel
`ORTHOGONAL_TOLERANCE_PX` and `snapNearOrthogonal` exist to hide the old
mismatch and can probably go now.

`STUB_LEN` was declared twice — `Port.tsx` draws the lead, `port-hit-test.ts`
decides where the wire attaches to it — and I changed one of them before
noticing, which would have put every wire four units short of its lead.

Junction dots were computed from waypoints only, which misses both real
cases: three wires meeting at a shared port (no wire has three vertices
anywhere) and a wire bending onto another's segment, which is how a rail is
tapped.

`examples/buck_converter.gsim_sch` is the reference sheet. Writing it
exposed that the canvas drops a wire naming a non-existent port in silence —
the diode's pins are `a`/`k`, the generator said `p`/`n`, and the render came
out missing its freewheel path. Now issue #2.

### The .vsix

Renaming the manifest changed the extension id and broke the integration
suite, which had the old literal in three more places. Centralising it
uncovered a nastier bug: `getTestApi` held its result in a local called
`exports`, and this file compiles to CommonJS where a module-level
`export const` is emitted as `exports.NAME` — so every reference to
`EXTENSION_ID` resolved to that local and hit its temporal dead zone.

`pnpm package` now builds, bundles the webviews into `media/frontend` (the
only root `webview-content.ts` probes that survives `vsce package`) and
writes the .vsix. Verified: it installs via `code --install-extension` into
an isolated extensions dir, and the integration suite passes against a real
VS Code 1.145.0 — **14 passing**. The kernel sidecar reaches `KERNEL_READY`
via `uv run --project . --package kernel-sidecar`.

### The port-domain bug

Generating the same buck converter through the new Python package produced
grey wires where the hand-written sheet produced black. Cause: 136 port
declarations across 55 metadata files said `kind: { domain: electrical }`.
That is `behavior.domain`'s enum, not `PortKindDomain`'s, where the value is
`physical-electrical`. Both fields are called `domain` and sit a few lines
apart in the same file, and nothing validated it — about a fifth of all
electrical parts drew in the unknown-domain grey and failed port-compatibility
against correctly-spelled parts. Fixed at source, manifest regenerated,
`catalog-port-domains.test.ts` added as the guard.

Worth noting how this was found: not by reading the metadata, but because an
independent implementation disagreed with the first one.

### Python front end

`general-simulator-python` (local, not yet pushed — repo creation was
blocked). `gsim.geometry` mirrors this repo's geometry contract exactly, and
the buck converter it generates renders identically to the hand-authored one.
It refuses what this canvas accepts and then draws wrongly: unknown port,
off-grid placement, overlapping bodies, diagonal wires. A real RC step
response through the engine matches `10*(1-exp(-t/RC))` to four figures.

### What's left

- Labels still anchor to the bbox rather than the ink, so a value sits
  further from a thin glyph than it should (issue #1).
- Issue #2: surface unresolvable wire endpoints as VS Code diagnostics.
- Custom symbols still do not persist — `saveSymbolToLibrary()` writes to the
  in-memory catalog only, and an authored symbol carries no behaviour.
- The docs still name commercial tools in ~30 places; that is a public-flip
  blocker, not a private-repo one.

### Rendering: a harness, and a first honest look

The user's primary complaint is that schematics render ugly. The checked-in
evidence (`docs/circuit_test/buck_converter/frontend_buck_schematic.png`,
June) shows diagonal wires, hairline strokes and stray stubs — but that
image is five months old and several fixes behind, so it is not evidence
about today's code.

`scripts/render-schematic.mjs` was written to end that problem: it boots the
Vite preview, drives the harness through `__gensim_load_document` and
`__gensim_canvasFitView`, and writes a 2× PNG of the canvas. Rendering
quality cannot be unit-tested — no assertion sees two overlapping labels —
so the rule (AGENTS.md invariant 6) is that rendering work is not done until
someone has looked at the output.

Rendering the buck converter on current code shows real progress since June:
**wires are orthogonal, strokes are firm and dark, junction dots are drawn.**
What is still wrong, from the render:

1. Terminal leads are far too long, so components float apart from the
   circuit instead of sitting in it. `Port.tsx`'s `STUB_LEN = 16` is in
   *screen* units, outside `BlockSymbol`'s `scale(SYMBOL_SCALE = 1.6)` —
   the two unit systems are not reconciled.
2. Pin names (`p`, `n`, `c+`, `c−`, `A`, `K`, `+`, `−`) are drawn on every
   terminal. A finished schematic never shows pin names on two-terminal
   passives.
3. Value labels sit far from their symbols — `100 µH` floats in open space —
   because `labelAnchorFor` anchors to the bbox, which is much larger than
   the drawn glyph.
4. No reference designators (`V1`, `L1`, `C1`, `R1`, `D1`).
5. The dotted grid is visually noisy at render density.
